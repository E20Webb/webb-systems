## I. Context  
### Modern Games Explain What Happened, Not Why

<div className="mt-8"></div>

Most tools can show:

- an event occurred  
- a state changed  
- a quest failed  
- an AI reacted  
- a balance issue emerged  

What they often cannot show is: **why**.

As systems grow more interconnected, behaviors emerge from chains of interactions spread across multiple systems. Understanding cause becomes increasingly difficult.

---

## II. The Problem  
### Complexity Obscures Causality

As worlds scale, teams spend significant time asking **“Why did this happen?”** instead of building new experiences.

- behaviors emerge from multiple systems  
- world‑state influences become difficult to track  
- balancing becomes increasingly opaque  
- debugging becomes investigation  
- designers lose confidence in outcomes  

---

## III. Philosophy  
### Principles That Enable Traceability

- **Visibility** — systems should be inspectable  
- **Determinism** — behavior should be reproducible  
- **Traceability** — outcomes should have clear origins  
- **Coherence** — cause and effect should remain aligned  
- **Designer Sovereignty** — intent should remain visible throughout development  

Understanding becomes a **property of the platform**.

### Traceability: The Missing Layer

Forge makes complex game worlds understandable by ensuring every behavior, state change, and systemic outcome can be traced back to its source.

---

## IV. Platform

## VI. Impact  
### Why Traceability Matters

When systems remain traceable:

- debugging accelerates  
- balancing becomes more reliable  
- world behavior becomes understandable  
- systemic contradictions become easier to identify  
- designers gain confidence in iteration  
- teams spend less time investigating and more time creating  

Traceability reduces complexity by **making complexity visible**.
