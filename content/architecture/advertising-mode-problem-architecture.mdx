During most of my twenties and early thirties, I worked in high-pressure sales for two Fortune 500 companies.

I was not responsible for national advertising.

I was responsible for something more immediate:

making sure people knew who I was, what I sold, and why they should care.

Once a week, I kept a fishbowl beside the cash register at a local restaurant.

Diners could drop in a business card or fill out a small raffle slip for a free lunch.

Over my own lunch break, I would come in, get the room’s attention, give a fifteen-second pitch, and draw names until I found someone who was actually there.

It worked because the giveaway created a reason to pay attention.

The pitch worked because it was brief.

The entire thing worked because it fit naturally into the environment instead of fighting it.

I used the same instinct everywhere else.

I mailed letters to leads I could not reach by phone.

Years later, in e-commerce, I learned how to advertise on TikTok without breaking the TikTok loop.

Different medium.

Same rule.

**If you weren’t advertising, you weren’t selling.**

But there was a second rule that mattered just as much:

**If the advertising broke the experience, you were doing it badly.**

That is what I keep thinking about when I look at console and PC gaming.

Gaming platforms have enormous amounts of attention.

But comparatively few ways to monetize that attention outside:

- storefronts
- subscriptions
- discounts
- DLC
- live-service monetization
- the games themselves

A discount tile does not create desire.

A marketplace does not automatically create discovery.

And a launcher cannot assume that because the customer arrived, the sale has already been earned.

In sales, I was never afforded the luxury of waiting for money to appear.

I had to pursue attention strategically.

I had to earn the business.

Gaming platforms should think the same way.

---

# THE WRONG QUESTION

The industry usually asks:

**How do we put more ads into games?**

That is the wrong question.

Players already know why they hate advertising in games.

It interrupts.

It delays.

It breaks immersion.

It often appears at the exact moment the player wants to do something else.

Traditional games are built around:

- pacing
- immersion
- agency
- progression
- tension
- reward
- continuity

Advertising inserted into those experiences feels foreign because it is foreign.

The game was not designed around it.

The ad was bolted on afterward.

```text
TRADITIONAL GAME
    +
RETROFITTED AD
    =
INTERRUPTION
```

That is not an advertising failure.

It is an architecture failure.

The problem is not that advertising exists.

The problem is that the revenue model does not belong to the mode of play.

---

# ADVERTISING IS A MODE PROBLEM

Different experiences can support different economic models.

Premium games do not need to behave like mobile games.

Competitive environments do not need to monetize like story campaigns.

Social feeds do not need to monetize like storefronts.

Short-form discovery does not need to monetize like a sixty-hour RPG.

The industry keeps trying to force a small set of business models across every type of experience.

That creates friction.

A better approach is structural:

**design the mode of play and the economic model together.**

```text
NEW MODE OF PLAY
    +
NATIVE ECONOMIC MODEL
    =
REVENUE WITHOUT DISRUPTION
```

That means the answer is not more advertising inside traditional games.

The answer is creating new play surfaces where advertising belongs.

---

# VALUE BEFORE ATTENTION

Mobile advertising taught an entire generation a new interaction grammar.

Short.

Swipeable.

Immediate.

Playable.

Disposable.

Familiar within seconds.

The problem is not the grammar.

The problem is how often that grammar is used coercively.

The ad interrupts.

The demo misrepresents the game.

The close button moves.

The reward is withheld.

The player is forced to sample something they did not ask for.

The lesson should not be:

**copy mobile ads.**

The lesson should be:

**separate the interaction grammar from the punishment.**

Younger players already understand:

- rapid sampling
- short-form discovery
- progression
- rewards
- seasonal structures
- instant transitions

Those behaviors can support a much better model.

One simple rule changes everything:

**Give the player value before asking for attention.**

Instead of:

```text
AD
    ↓
MAYBE VALUE
```

build:

```text
PLAY
    ↓
PLAY
    ↓
DISCOVER
    ↓
PLAY
    ↓
NATURAL BREAK
    ↓
AD
```

The ad stops functioning as a toll gate.

It becomes part of a larger rhythm.

# PLAYABLE DISCOVERY

The first opportunity is playable discovery.

Today, discovery is usually passive.

A player sees:

- a trailer
- a store tile
- a recommendation
- a discount
- a review
- a creator clip

Then they decide whether to investigate further.

But games are interactive products.

The most natural advertisement for a game is often the game itself.

That suggests a different discovery model:

```text
DISCOVERY
    ↓
PLAYABLE SAMPLE
    ↓
CURIOSITY
    ↓
ANOTHER SAMPLE
    ↓
INTEREST
    ↓
ACTION
```

Sampling becomes gameplay.

The player is invited to discover rather than forced to watch.

The experience can remain:

- short
- frictionless
- cloud-native
- varied
- low-commitment
- easy to enter
- easy to leave

The economic principle is simple:

**Monetize curiosity, not frustration.**

Traditional mobile ads often interrupt one game to advertise another.

A better system turns discovery itself into something worth choosing.

**The best ad surface may be an experience worth playing.**

---

# PROGRESSION CAN MAKE DISCOVERY FEEL LIKE PLAY

Discovery usually resets.

A player samples something, leaves, and starts over somewhere else.

But modern players already understand progression systems deeply.

XP.

Tiers.

Challenges.

Seasonal rewards.

Unlocks.

Shared goals.

That suggests another architectural move:

**discovery itself can become progressive.**

```text
TRY
    ↓
EARN
    ↓
PROGRESS
    ↓
UNLOCK
    ↓
SHARE
    ↓
REPEAT
```

The important idea is not to turn every recommendation into a grind.

It is to acknowledge that discovery can carry continuity.

A player can move through many experiences while still feeling that their time belongs to one larger journey.

That changes discovery from promotion into participation.

---

# COMPETITIVE SPECTACLE

Playable discovery is not the only mode that can support native advertising.

Competition has its own economic grammar.

Sports have understood this for decades.

Spectacle creates natural commercial boundaries:

- before an event
- between rounds
- around brackets
- during highlights
- inside sponsorship packages
- around rivalry
- around seasonal competition

These moments do not have to interrupt the competition.

They can frame it.

```text
COMPETITION
│
├── EVENTS
├── RIVALRIES
├── LADDERS
├── HIGHLIGHTS
├── CREATOR CHALLENGES
└── SPONSORSHIP
```

The monetization model is different.

Playable discovery monetizes curiosity.

Competitive environments can monetize spectacle.

Sponsored tournaments.

Featured events.

Creator-hosted challenges.

Branded competitive moments.

Seasonal rivalries.

None of these require an ad to appear in the middle of traditional gameplay.

The mode itself creates the commercial surface.

---

# CULTURE CAN BECOME A COMMERCE SURFACE

There is another place where gaming platforms leave economic value on the table:

culture.

Games generate a constant stream of:

- clips
- highlights
- creator posts
- seasonal moments
- esports moments
- community discoveries
- launches
- updates
- viral moments

Most of that cultural attention exists somewhere else.

Social media captures it.

Video platforms capture it.

Creator platforms capture it.

The gaming platform often owns the game but not the moment around the game.

That is a structural gap.

A live cultural surface can connect attention to action without interrupting gameplay.

```text
CULTURAL MOMENT
    ↓
INTEREST
    ↓
CONTEXT
    ↓
ACTION
```

The action might be:

- learn more
- install
- buy
- join
- watch
- follow
- jump in

The important distinction is that commerce follows interest.

It does not interrupt play.

Culture becomes the discovery surface.

Action becomes native to the moment.

# THREE ECONOMIC SURFACES

These models reveal a broader platform opportunity.

```text
NATIVE AD ARCHITECTURE
│
├── PLAYABLE DISCOVERY
│     Curiosity
│     Sampling
│     Progression
│     Natural breaks
│
├── COMPETITIVE SPECTACLE
│     Events
│     Sponsorship
│     Rivalry
│     Cultural moments
│
└── LIVE CULTURE
        Highlights
        Creators
        Discovery
        Commerce actions
```

These are not three ways to inject ads into games.

They are three modes in which advertising can become structurally compatible with the experience.

That distinction matters.

# TRADITIONAL GAMES SHOULD BE LEFT ALONE

This is the part that should be simple.

Traditional players do not want:

- interruptions
- forced demos
- mobile-style coercion
- ads inserted into premium experiences
- monetization systems that distort difficulty
- progression designed around frustration

They should not get any of that.

Premium games should remain premium games.

Story-driven games should protect pacing.

Competitive games should protect competitive integrity.

Single-player games should protect immersion.

Existing experiences should not be degraded to support a new revenue model.

The platform should create new surfaces instead.

**New revenue should come from new modes of play, not from breaking old ones.**

---

# THE RULES

If advertising becomes part of a gaming platform, the architecture should enforce clear boundaries.

## 1. NEVER INTERRUPT HIGH-INTENSITY PLAY

Advertising belongs at natural boundaries.

Not during action.

Not during emotional peaks.

Not between input and response.

## 2. VALUE COMES FIRST

The player should receive meaningful entertainment, discovery, or utility before attention is monetized.

## 3. ADS SHOULD BE LEGIBLE

Advertising should not depend on deception.

The player should understand when something is sponsored.

## 4. MONETIZATION SHOULD NOT DISTORT GAMEPLAY

Difficulty should not increase to create ad demand.

Progression should not be deliberately slowed to sell relief.

## 5. THE PLAYER SHOULD HAVE A CLEAN EXIT

Ad-supported experiences should not depend on traps.

Participation should remain voluntary.

## 6. ATTENTION IS A FINITE RESOURCE

More ads do not automatically create more value.

Saturation destroys the surface.

## 7. THE MODE SHOULD JUSTIFY THE MODEL

If advertising feels foreign to the experience, the architecture is wrong.

# THE PLATFORM OPPORTUNITY

Gaming is one of the largest attention economies in the world.

But platforms still monetize that attention through a comparatively narrow set of mechanisms.

That does not mean they should become ad networks.

It means they should become more thoughtful about the relationship between:

**play**  
**discovery**  
**culture**  
**competition**  
**commerce**

These do not need to exist as separate systems.

They can reinforce one another.

A player discovers something because it is fun to sample.

A community becomes interested because a competitive event creates cultural energy.

A highlight creates curiosity.

Curiosity creates action.

Action creates play.

Play creates another cultural moment.

```text
PLAY
    ↓
CULTURE
    ↓
DISCOVERY
    ↓
COMMERCE
    ↓
MORE PLAY
```

That is a healthier economic loop than interruption.

# XBOX FOREVER

Xbox Forever explores this larger idea:

**new modes of play can support new revenue models without degrading the games people already love.**

And it does not require games to be rebuilt around the system.

Back catalogs remain valuable.

Current games remain compatible.

New games do not need to adopt a new monetization model.

Instead, the platform creates new modes of play around the games that already exist — using their worlds, systems, competition, culture, and playable moments as raw material for new experiences.

```text
EXISTING GAME CATALOG
│
├── BACK CATALOG
├── CURRENT GAMES
└── NEW RELEASES
        ↓
PLATFORM MODES
        ↓
NEW EXPERIENCES
        ↓
NEW DISCOVERY
NEW CULTURE
NEW REVENUE
```

The game remains the game.

The platform creates additional ways to experience it.

That distinction is critical.

Xbox Forever does not need every studio to redesign its product around advertising.

It gives the platform new surfaces where:

- discovery is playable
- competition creates spectacle
- culture creates commerce
- attention is earned
- existing games gain new context
- traditional gaming remains untouched

A twenty-year-old game can participate.

A game released today can participate.

A future game can participate.

The architecture creates new value around the catalog rather than requiring the catalog to conform to the architecture.

That is the real opportunity.

Not more ads.

Not games redesigned around ads.

**New modes of play built around games people already want to play.**

Better architecture.

Because advertising does not have to break the game.

**It has to belong to the mode.**
